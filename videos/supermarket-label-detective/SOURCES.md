# Evidence and asset notes

Reviewed 22 September 2026. Fictional packages; illustrative comparisons. This film teaches label reading, not whether a particular product is healthy or unhealthy.

- **Natural is not a nutrition score.** FDA explicitly distinguishes its natural policy from nutritional or health benefit: https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/use-term-natural-food-labeling . The film does not claim that natural is unregulated or meaningless worldwide.
- **Multigrain does not guarantee whole grains.** NDSU Extension, *Make at least Half Your Grains Whole Grains*, p. 1, explains checking ingredients, including whole wheat and whole oats: https://www.ndsu.edu/agriculture/sites/default/files/2022-03/fn726.pdf . Only factual concepts used; no artwork or copied prose.
- **No added sugar and sugar-free are different.** American Heart Association: https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/healthy-eating/eat-smart/sugar/difference-between-sugar-free-and-no-added-sugar . FSSAI also illustrates no-added-sugar juice with naturally occurring sugars: https://fssai-digest.fssai.gov.in/assets/complete-magazine.pdf . The film states no jurisdiction-specific numerical legal threshold.
- **Compare equal amounts and consider actual portions.** FDA label guidance: https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/how-understand-and-use-nutrition-facts-label . Worked example is arithmetic: 6g sugar / 30g food = 8g sugar / 40g food = 20g / 100g. This comparison says nothing about the foods' other nutrients.
